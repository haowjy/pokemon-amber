# \[Chapter 17.5\] Warning Signs

The laboratory was empty except for Professor Oak.

Night pressed against the tall windows. Machines hummed along the walls, and the shelves between them were packed with books and carefully labeled Pokeballs. Oak sat beneath the laboratory's enormous computer screen, turning a small vial between his fingers. The last drops of Fuji's pink serum clung to the bottom.

The computer gave a sharp electronic chime.

Oak pressed a key with his free hand to answer the call. A young man sat straight-backed on the other side, wild red hair rising above sharp, tired eyes. He wore a dark high-collared coat edged in gold over a red, diamond-patterned front. His broad navy cape spilled around the chair, exposing flashes of its pale lining.

"Professor Oak."

"Lance," Oak replied. "You certainly took your time calling me back."

Lance scratched the back of his head. "My apologies. I've been rather busy these past few weeks tracing strange psychic disturbances across Kanto. I'm due in Sinnoh tomorrow as well, and half of Indigo still seems to be waiting for me to call back."

Oak studied him with a grin. "Being Champion isn't easy, is it?"

Lance laughed. "Ha! No, it isn't. I still don't know how you ever managed without a clan behind you."

Oak's grin faded. He looked down at his hands. His fingers had begun to tremble.

"I didn't," he said quietly. He steadied his hands and looked back at Lance. "But that isn't why I called."

Lance's expression sobered.

"There was an attack in Pallet Town. One of my good assistants was killed," Oak continued. "And the killer didn't hesitate to face me either. He could match me."

Lance frowned. "Your team may be locked up, but even so..."

Oak lifted the vial. "He injected this into a Magmar. It looked almost like another evolution. Do you know anything about it? Perhaps... a certain pet project of the Indigo League?"

Lance gave a strained chuckle. "An evolution for Magmar... I can't say I've heard of anything like it."

Oak lowered the vial. "I hope not."

Neither man spoke for several seconds.

Oak was the first to break the silence. He lowered his head and sighed. "The attack seemed personally motivated. I just fear it might not be the last we see of this thing."

"Who attacked you?" Lance asked.

"Tenma Fuji. He was a Celadon University researcher before the war."

Lance sat forward. "A Kantoan scientist, then. I'll note it down. Send over the sample. I'll have League scientists examine it."

"Thank you. I'll send it tonight."

Oak turned the vial between his fingers. "There is one other matter."

Lance waited.

"I won't ask you to confirm anything about the project I mentioned. But between this serum, the increasing natural disasters, and the rise in crime, I would like to formally request the League's permission to reassemble my old team."

"No."

Oak raised his eyes to the screen.

"The terms of your pardon have not changed."

Oak held his gaze for a moment, then sighed. "I understand."

"Is that all?"

Oak nodded.

"Then goodbye, Oak." Lance paused. When he continued, some of the coldness had left his voice. "I'm sorry for your loss."

Oak lowered his head.

Oak vanished from the screen, leaving Lance with his own dim reflection. He sat looking at it for several seconds, one hand curled into a fist. Then his shoulders sagged, and he sank into his chair.

"No one told me being Champion meant talking to so many people all night."

Dragonite gave a low rumble beside him.

"At least Oak had something interesting to say."

The call list reappeared beneath Oak's name. Twenty-eight still waiting, with the Council of Agriculture next.

Lance uncurled his hand and selected it. "This won't be nearly as interesting."

A stern-faced official appeared and began explaining a dispute over Tauros grazing routes.

The grazing dispute took one call off the list. Lance worked through several more before giving up for the night. A few hours after speaking to Oak, he finally reached his room and collapsed face-first onto the bed, his cape still fastened around his shoulders.

"I didn't finish," he mumbled into the blankets. "Why did they all answer this late? Damn workaholics."

He was asleep before Dragonite nudged the door open behind him.

Dragonite nudged Lance's shoulder.

He buried his face deeper in the blankets.

Dragonite nudged him again.

"Five more minutes."

Dragonite growled and pointed toward the clock beside the bed.

Lance opened one eye.

Then both.

"Why didn't you wake me sooner?"

Dragonite stared at him.

Lance was still fastening his cape when Dragonite launched from the terrace.

The sun was already high when Sinnoh's coast appeared beneath them.

Dragonite landed outside the Sinnoh League's conference wing. Lance threw open the meeting-room doors, and every head around the long table turned toward him.

Lance's eyes swept across the room. Pryce. Palmer. A Blackthorn elder. Officers and League representatives from four regions.

Lance stopped inside the doorway and bowed to the room. "Lance, Champion of Indigo. Please forgive my late arrival."

At the head of the table, an old officer gave him a long look. Her faded blue hair was gathered into two short loops above her ears, and deep wrinkles lined her face.

Palmer caught Lance's eye, then rose and bowed.

"On behalf of the Sinnoh League, welcome to Sinnoh," he said. "We are honored to host these negotiations. I would also like to formally congratulate you on becoming Champion, though my congratulations are rather late."

Lance returned the bow. "Thank you, Champion Palmer."

Palmer sat, and Lance took the last empty chair.

The old officer tapped a control. Harvest totals, food reserves, and current prices from each region filled the screen.

"Let's begin with food supplies and trade routes. Production is falling. Prices are rising. Several of our regions have already begun rationing food, and some warn that famine will follow."

Photographs of farmland replaced the figures. Neat rows of crops ended against warped forests, stone corridors, and stretches of land that did not belong in the country surrounding them.

"The primary cause is the increasing number of Mystery Dungeons. They are swallowing fields and roads, forcing caravans onto longer routes, and opening closer to cities. Rangers from each region contained two outbreaks near city limits last month."

"The second issue is increased legendary and mythical Pokemon activity. Johto reports Raikou, Entei, and Suicune near settlements. Hoenn and Sinnoh report sightings of legendary giants emerging near ancient ruins. Kanto reports an unidentified Psychic type with power comparable to a legendary. Strong and rare Pokemon are moving outside their usual territories as well."

"Why are you saying all this? Are these incidents connected?" an official asked.

The old officer paused. "According to several professors, legendaries roam when the world is out of balance, and the increase in Mystery Dungeons is another symptom. From what we know, these are connected."

Grainy photographs appeared behind her: crowds beyond police lines, trainers camped along mountain paths, distant figures in dark uniforms marked with a red **R**.

"Whatever the cause, people are making the situation worse. These sightings are drawing trainers, collectors, trading guilds, and commercial crews from across the regions. We have caught some deliberately causing destruction to draw out already agitated legendaries and rare Pokemon. We don't know how much of the wider disturbance is man-made."

"Do we know of anyone who has successfully captured any of the legendaries?" someone asked.

"An explorer named Brandon has captured one of the three giants. The Hoenn League is already in direct contact with him."

She waited for the murmurs to die down. Incident reports from ports, roads, and city precincts filled the screen.

"Aside from people attempting to capture legendaries, reports of smuggling, poaching, Pokemon theft, and banditry have all increased."

Several hooded figures appeared in white tabards over close-fitting dark clothes.

"A Pokemon-liberation movement with origins traced to Unova has begun attracting followers in several regions. Its more fanatical adherents steal Pokemon from their trainers and release them into the wild, claiming to free them."

The next photographs showed protesters surrounding a Mystery Dungeon survey team beneath painted banners. In a smaller panel, equipment cases lay overturned beside shattered instruments.

"In Hoenn, a growing environmental movement claims the disasters are nature's answer to human expansion. Most of its members limit themselves to peaceful protest. More radical factions have blocked Mystery Dungeon surveys and route-clearing crews, destroyed equipment, or interfered with relief work."

"Are they working together?" someone asked.

"We have no evidence that any of these groups are coordinating. Treat them as separate problems."

The old officer moved through the remaining evidence: villages abandoned near new Mystery Dungeons, Pokemon Centers short of medicine, caravans vanishing where roads crossed from one jurisdiction into another.

She switched off the screen. "For generations, the Jenny branches protected the Joy clan's healers as they traveled. Once permanent Pokemon Centers were established, our branches adopted local jurisdictions. That model is no longer enough. We must cooperate instead of fighting among ourselves."

"The treaty before you would establish the International Police. Jenny officers assigned from each branch would form its first regional network alongside investigators appointed by every member League."

She placed both hands on the table. "It would also bind our regions into a permanent alliance for trade, disaster response, and mutual defense. Each League would keep its own government and laws. But when a shipment, crime, disaster, or attack crosses a border, all four would recognize the same interregional law. We would coordinate protection of roads and shipping, and share personnel, intelligence, food, medicine, and other emergency reserves."

"Over the next several days, we must decide what each region is obligated to contribute, who may act across which borders, how disputes are judged, and how the alliance is funded."

The negotiations consumed the next several days and nearly stretched into a week. When the final session adjourned late that afternoon, the treaty bore the seals of all four Leagues. The alliance and its International Police now existed. Turning either into something that worked would take much longer.

On the return flight, forest rolled peacefully beneath Dragonite's wings.

They crossed into Kanto beneath a fading sky. As Viridian slid below Dragonite's left wing, the western edge of the city flashed white.

Several seconds later, the sound of the explosion reached them.

Lance frowned as he leaned over the saddle. "Down!"

Hours earlier, Dr. Fuji sat alone on a narrow bunk. Bandages wrapped his chest, one arm, and half his face; above them, one eye had swollen nearly shut. Dried blood darkened the collar of his brand-new orange jumpsuit.

Most of the cell had already fallen into darkness. The last of the afternoon sun slipped through a narrow window high in the wall, laying long golden bars across the floor.

The iron bars rattled open.

Fuji didn't react.

Two men stepped through the golden streaks of afternoon light. The first wore a finely tailored charcoal suit with a deep red tie. A Persian moved at his side, its pale shoulder nearly brushing his leg. The second was older and bald, with a thick white mustache and round dark glasses. His lab coat hung open over a dark shirt and a flame-patterned tie, the dying sunlight burning across his lenses.

As the shadows stretched into the cell, Fuji finally reacted and lifted his head. "Giovanni... Blaine..." he said weakly.

Blaine moved to the wall opposite the bunk and crossed his arms.

Giovanni stopped in front of Fuji, his shadow blocking the sunlight from Fuji's face.

"Why did you attack Pallet Town?"

Fuji's good hand tightened around the edge of the bunk.

"The clone was a failure."

Giovanni didn't respond and let Fuji continue.

"The research was perfect. Every DNA sequence. Every measurement. I reconstructed her exactly as she had been. She looked like her. She sounded like her. Even the birthmark beneath her left shoulder was in precisely the right place. Her brain was the same."

He looked down at his open hand.

"But she held her chopsticks incorrectly. She hated the dresses her mother made for her. She stared at the cartoons she used to love as if she had never seen them before." His fingers curled. "She spoke like someone else. Sometimes she looked at me as if I were the stranger."

"But why Pallet Town?" Giovanni asked.

Fuji looked up quickly. "Delia... Her mother... I thought... I thought we could be a family again..."

"But..." Fuji stared at the window. "She chose that lab assistant. A stupid lab assistant over me. Over her own daughter. And then they corrupted her. They corrupted my sweet little Amber and filled her with evil thoughts."

Giovanni waited.

The words came faster.

"She blamed me," Fuji said. "She looked at me as if I had abandoned them. But I was serving Kanto. Chimera was supposed to end the war. I left them because I thought it would keep them safe." His voice caught. "When I came home, Amber was dead. Johto called our surrender peace, and Delia looked at me as though I had killed our daughter myself."

His good hand struck his knee.

"I tried to bring her back on my own. I tried until there was nothing left."

"Just get to the point alrea---"

Giovanni raised one hand.

Blaine stopped. His jaw worked beneath his mustache, but he said nothing more.

"Continue," Giovanni said. "You tried until nothing was left?"

Fuji looked between them. "Yes... Then you found me. You gave me the funding I needed. But even still, Giovanni, even still..."

"Can't she see how much time and effort I've put in? How much I love her... How much I love them both... Why..." Fuji's teeth clicked shut. "What's the point? Even if I clone another Amber, she wouldn't be the same. It wouldn't be the same... without... Delia..."

"Why not replace Delia?"

"..." Fuji glanced up at Giovanni before he shook his head. "I could recreate Delia's body. But her mind? Her memories? How do you clone memories?"

Giovanni stepped back with a faint smile.

Fuji sat in silence.

At last Fuji said, "Memories... are they even cells...?"

Giovanni's brow furrowed at the final mutter before he turned to Blaine.

"Let's just make him disappear for now."

Fuji was shaken from his musings. "G-Giovanni..."

Giovanni walked out without answering. Persian followed at his side.

Blaine watched them leave, then drew a Pokeball from inside his coat. "That Magmar was mine," he said.

The Pokeball opened. White light flooded the cell, and a Magmar took shape between them. Its flames drove back the darkness and turned the golden bars on the floor red.

Blaine removed his glasses and looked down at Fuji.

"And you wasted him on a stupid tantrum."

Magmar crossed the cell. Fuji backed into the bunk, his good hand rising too late. One hot claw closed around his cheek. Another pressed beneath his swollen eye.

"I won't make this quick," Blaine said.

The detention block was still collapsing when Dragonite struck the road. Smoke rolled across the street as another section of the roof folded inward, carrying a shower of sparks into the evening sky. Somewhere inside, an alarm began to shriek. People stumbled from the neighboring buildings. Others stood frozen where the blast had thrown them.

Lance dropped from Dragonite's back. An officer staggered through the smoke with one sleeve smoldering. She saw him and tried to straighten.

"C-Champion Lance!"

"What happened?"

"I don't know! T-the east wing---"

The building groaned behind her.

"Dragonite, Twister! Blow away the fire!"

Dragonite beat its wings. A spiraling wind tore through the shattered entrance and drove the flames back into the ruined block. Smoke and embers climbed above the rooftops.

As the smoke cleared, something moved at the edge of Lance's vision. A man's shadow slipped around the corner of the building and into a narrow alley. Lance ran after it.

He rounded the corner seconds later and stopped. The alley ended at a windowless brick wall. There were no doors, no fire escapes, and no sign of the man. Only ash turning slowly in Dragonite's wind.

"Viridian is only one route north of Pallet Town..." Lance muttered as he looked back toward the burning detention block.

"Could it be...?"
