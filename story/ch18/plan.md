# Chapter 18 Plan: Departure (Working Title)

**POV:** Amber (1st person)
**Timeline:** Amber's last day in Pallet before her journey
**Location:** Pallet Town — school, Oak's lab, Ketchum house, Route 1

---

## Purpose

Transition from Arc 0 to Arc 1. Amber leaves Pallet Town calmly, legitimately, and with Oak's full support. This is NOT a trauma-driven flight. It's the end of a year of waiting — a 23-year-old mind finally getting permission to leave a situation that's been politely suffocating her.

The chapter should breathe. Quiet, understated, voice-forward. Sets the tonal register for Arc 1: lighter than late Arc 0, humor returning, but with earned depth underneath.

---

## Scene 1: Last Day of School

**Entry:** Amber hunched over her notebook at her desk, arm curled protectively around it. She's studying — moves, stats, timeline fragments, whatever she can reconstruct from her past-life game/anime knowledge. The posture itself tells you what she looks like to her classmates every day: the weird quiet girl with the notebook she won't show anyone. This is why she's an outcast. Three-four years of this.

**The notebook:** Amber keeps a private notebook of everything she remembers — Pokemon stats, move lists, timelines, future events. She studies it religiously. She's extremely protective of it because the contents are inexplicable (how would a 13-year-old know this?). The notebook is her edge AND her vulnerability. Possible interlude material: semi-AI-generated doodles/notes from its pages released between chapters.

**The interruption:** She didn't invite this. A loud kid — the type who announces things — wanders up and says something like "Yo, is it true Oak is sponsoring you?" Loud enough that the room hears. The weird notebook girl is suddenly the most interesting person in the class. Amber's first instinct: snap the notebook shut. Physical guard up before social guard.

**The swarm:** Now everyone's crowding around. Energy is jealousy + curiosity, not awe. They're stuck in school for another year and she's already going.
- "How'd you get approved early?"
- "What's on the exam?" (they're all prepping for it next year)
- "What's your starter?" / journey-fantasy questions
- Questions that reveal THEIR assumptions, not her reality
- The questions pile up — people talking over each other. Amber gives minimal answers while internally cataloguing with dry commentary.

**What this shows:**
- The notebook explains her isolation (always hunched over something secret, never engaging) — no exposition needed
- No real connections. The only reason they're talking to her NOW is novelty.
- The disconnect: she's mentally 23, can't say it, and her annoyance is slightly too dry for a 13-year-old
- What she's leaving behind is thin. This is WHY leaving is easy.

**What Amber feels:** Annoyed. Not sad, not nostalgic. These people weren't interested in HER — they're interested in the news. Her guard is up (notebook) and her patience is low. Externally minimal, internally sharp.

**Ditto:** Not at school. Pokemon not allowed (or at least not hers). She's alone here — no partner, no buffer. School-Amber is isolated in a way journey-Amber won't be.

**Exit:** She leaves. Not dramatically — the crowd dissipates because she's boring to talk to, or she just walks out mid-question. No goodbye, no sentiment. Notebook back in her bag, close to her body. School evaporates from her mind. She's already thinking about what's next.

**Length:** Short. 1-2 pages max. Punchy, voice-forward. One job: this is what she's leaving, and it's nothing.

**Craft notes (writing-principles):**
- Trust the reader: don't explain the disconnection or loneliness. The notebook posture + minimal answers + fast exit do it.
- Economy: classmate questions do double duty (reveal their assumptions AND show Amber's non-engagement).
- Social simulation: Amber's annoyance through behavior and narration rhythm, not labels.
- Don't summarize feelings at scene end. Don't reflect. She just goes.

## Scene 2: Oak's Lab

**Entry:** Amber goes straight to Oak's lab after school. This is her actual home base — where she's comfortable, where Ditto is, where she spends her real time. She technically lives at Delia's (forged documents = legal daughter) but in practice she's barely there. Comes home late, leaves early. Her life is at the lab.

**Ditto:** Here. Greets her. The one relationship that's fully easy. A small beat of reunion/comfort before the Oak conversation.

**Setting:** Afternoon, ~3pm. The lab is familiar — but also slightly different lately. Oak's been busy. New equipment, locked rooms, papers Amber's seen but never asks about. He's been traveling more, meeting with people she doesn't know. The lab has traces of work she doesn't understand.

**Environmental seed:** Somewhere in the lab — on a desk, in a case, under a light — a stone. Something that catches light oddly. Amber might glance at it, note it's new, not think about it further. (This is a Mega Stone. She doesn't know that. Neither does the reader yet. It connects to the serum investigation: Oak's been working with the Champion on what Fuji's serum was, which led to government-sponsored secret mega evolution research. The Viridian cave ore from the Route 1 incident will connect later.)

**Oak's recent busyness (context, not exposition):**
- Since Ch17, Oak has been pulled into high-level work: investigating the pink serum Fuji used on Magmar, working with Lance (Champion), government-sponsored mega evolution research
- This is why he hasn't mediated between Amber and Delia — genuinely consumed, not neglectful by choice
- Amber's had run of the lab but not much of his focused attention
- Him being fully present TODAY is deliberate and noticeable. He chose this.

**What Oak provides:** Gear/funds have been handled over weeks of preparation. This isn't the supply handoff — that's already done. Maybe confirming a final detail (Pokedex? Oak's Letter?) but the practical stuff is background noise.

**The Sam subtext (invisible to Amber, visible to attentive readers):**
- Oak usually advocates STAYING in school — breadth of knowledge, understanding, science before journey. He's famous for this.
- He's letting Amber skip a year. This breaks his own rules.
- He's too relaxed about it. Too certain. His ease is slightly disproportionate to what she's shown as a 13-year-old.
- He's already seen her in action — 40 years ago, as young Sam. He can't say this.
- His certainty reads as trust that hasn't been fully earned in the visible timeline. The gap is the tell.

**The conversation shape:**
1. Amber arrives. Ditto. Comfort. Oak is there, casual.
2. The "responsibility speech" — but truncated. He starts the version he gives every departing trainer and then... skips ahead? Cuts it short? Says "you know all this already" in a way that's slightly too knowing? He doesn't belabor what she clearly doesn't need.
3. The real beat: something that comes from his Sam memory without revealing it. NOT mystical, NOT heavy. Maybe:
   - Something about trusting what she notices about Pokemon (practical advice that's secretly about her Mew DNA capacity)
   - Something about plans breaking ("the journey won't go the way you think" — said too certainly, like someone who KNOWS)
   - Something about coming back different ("you won't be the same person when you come home" — said as if he's seen it)
4. A brief moment where Amber catches something odd in his manner — a look that's too warm, too familiar, too certain — and doesn't know what to do with it. She files it away without interpreting it.

**The pivot:** Oak asks, "Are you going to see Delia?" Not a command. A question. Amber hesitates. Slowly nods. Oak puts down whatever he's holding, stands up. He's coming with her. He doesn't ask if she wants company — he just does it. This says everything about what that visit requires.

**Craft notes:**
- Trust the reader: the Sam subtext is in BEHAVIOR (ease, certainty, odd warmth, truncated speech) not in narration explaining "something about his gaze suggested..."
- Economy: Oak's dialogue does triple duty — surface advice + Sam subtext + character (who he is as a mentor)
- Don't make the subtext too loud. Amber doesn't understand it. First-time readers won't catch it. Re-readers will.
- The pivot to "are you going to see Delia" is the scene's real work. Everything before is warmth; this is the obligation.

## Scene 3: Delia's House

**Entry:** Oak and Amber walk to Delia's. (Pallet is small — everything is walking distance.) Ditto with them. The walk itself might be a brief beat — Oak filling the silence, or comfortable quiet.

**The living situation (context, not exposition):**
- Amber technically lives here. The forged documents make her Delia's legal daughter.
- In practice: functional avoidance. Amber sleeps here but is barely present. Delia leaves clean towels, makes food Amber might eat, keeps things running — but never sits with her. They orbit the same house without connecting.
- It's not cold or cruel. It's the best either of them can manage.
- Delia can't fully relax around Amber. A flinch when she enters a room. Performing kindness without warmth. 3-4 years of this.

**Why Delia can't land:**
- Amber is connected to everything terrible (Fuji, fire, Stephen's death)
- Amber said "I'm not your daughter" in an adult voice at age 10 — Delia heard or witnessed that. Something about this child is *off*.
- Amber looks like the original Amber who died of cancer at 4 — Delia's childhood friend's daughter
- The forged documents trapped Delia in legal responsibility for someone she didn't choose and can't quite face

**What happens:**
- They arrive. Delia is home. There's a moment of... adjustment. Delia registering that this is happening — the goodbye, the departure, the end of this arrangement.
- Oak is the buffer. His presence makes it possible for both of them to be in the room.
- Delia does something kind but can't quite land it — food? A small practical gift for the journey? "Be safe"? The gesture is real but the delivery is stiff. She's trying.
- Amber receives it without knowing how to respond. Not ungrateful — just aware that this is performance on both sides.

**Ash breaks the tension:**
- Ash is home (12 years old now, no Pokemon since Poliwag died).
- He's excited — not about Amber leaving, about the JOURNEY. Same energy as the school kids but the difference is Amber GIVES BACK to Ash. She engages. She has warmth here.
- Ash instantly gravitates to Ditto. Wants to play. Ditto is fun, Ash loves Pokemon, this is simple and good.
- The contrast: Delia stiff and performing → Ash loose and real. Amber mirrors whoever she's talking to — minimal with Delia, warm with Ash.

**The Ash jealousy beat (folds in here):**
- "You're leaving?! I have TWO MORE YEARS!"
- Amber: "I don't even want to be Pokemon Master."
- Ash: incomprehension. What else would you DO?
- Comedy, warmth, the one uncomplicated relationship.

**What's NOT said (between Amber and Delia):**
- "I'm glad you're going" / "This was never going to work"
- "You made my life harder by existing" / "I know, I'm sorry"
- "You look like her" / "I'm not her"
- All of this lives in the avoidance pattern, not in dialogue.

**Exit:** The goodbye between Amber and Delia is minimal. Maybe just a "goodbye" and a nod. Or Delia manages one slightly real thing — a touch on the shoulder, a sentence that's almost honest — before retreating. It's not cathartic. It's just... over. The arrangement ends.

Ash's goodbye is louder, warmer, and probably involves Ditto doing something funny.

**Craft notes:**
- The Delia beat is SHORT. Don't wallow in the awkwardness — a few precisely observed moments do more than a long scene.
- Ash provides relief AND contrast. His ease highlights Delia's inability.
- Trust the reader to feel the Delia dynamic from behavior alone. No narration explaining "things had always been awkward between us."
- The cookie from Oak (if he brings one, or if there are cookies at Delia's) can echo Ch12 without pointing at it. Same house (rebuilt), different people, different weight.
- Ditto playing with Ash = uncomplicated joy. A breath before the quieter final scenes.

## Scene 3: Return Home — Awkward Delia

Amber comes home from Oak's lab. Delia is there. The scene runs on what is NOT said.

**The situation:** Delia is legally responsible for Amber (forged documents). They've lived together for a year+. It's been... fine. Polite. Awkward. Delia can't look at Amber without seeing the dead daughter, Fuji, Stephen's murder, the house fire. Amber can't pretend to be the kid Delia needs her to be.

**What happens:** A greeting. Maybe Delia has prepared something (food? a small gift for the journey?). The kindness is real but the connection isn't. Both of them know Amber leaving makes everyone's life easier, and neither will say it.

**What Amber feels:** Not guilt. Not relief exactly. Something quieter — awareness that this is the last time she'll stand in this kitchen performing normalcy for someone else's benefit.

**Length:** Short. This scene lives in the silences.

## Scene 4: The Ranch

**Setting:** Oak's ranch, late afternoon. Amber goes back to the lab/ranch after the brief Delia arrival. This is the warm, physical, light part of the chapter.

**What happens:**
- Amber says goodbye to the ranch Pokemon. She's known them for years — this is where she's spent her real time.
- Gary is there doing chores, annoyed as hell. Last day of school and he still has to muck stalls or whatever Oak makes him do. Mouthy about it.
- Ash tags along (followed Amber, wants to be near Ditto and the action).
- Ditto plays with ranch Pokemon — transforms into one, comedy beat. Light and fun.

**The jealousy beat lives here (both boys are 12, both stuck waiting):**
- Ash: "You're leaving?! I have TWO MORE YEARS!"
- Gary: also jealous but won't admit it — channels it into unsolicited advice and criticism
- Amber: "I don't even want to be Pokemon Master."
- Ash: genuine incomprehension. What else would you DO?
- Gary and Ash argue about what THEY'LL do — Amber is already forgotten as they bicker
- Comedy, warmth. These are Amber's actual people.

**Tone:** The lightest scene in the chapter. Physical, noisy, Pokemon everywhere, kids arguing. Amber is engaged and giving energy back — unlike school, unlike Delia's.

## Scene 5: Dinner at Delia's

**Setting:** Evening. Delia's kitchen/dining room. A big meal.

**Who's there:** Delia, Oak, Amber, Ash, Gary, Ditto.

**The surface:** Lively, noisy, warm. Delia cooked — Oak praises the food, everyone digs in. This is a proper send-off meal even if nobody calls it that.

**Chatter topics (the noise that fills the room):**
- Which gym should Amber challenge first? (Gary has STRONG opinions)
- Pokemon type debates ("Water beats Fire, that's basic" / "Not if—")
- Route 1 speculation/rumors
- Gary insisting he'd do everything better when his turn comes / still salty about chores
- Ash and Gary arguing (rivals-in-waiting energy, both 12, both jealous)
- Oak dropping dry comments, mostly listening, enjoying the table
- Journey strategy, what to pack, what Pokemon she might catch
- Amber actually engaged — debating, answering, warm

**The Delia beat (the scene's real work):**
- Delia cooked. Delia served. Delia sat.
- At some point during the chatter, Delia quietly leaves the table.
- Amber doesn't notice the moment. The table just has one fewer person.
- Nobody acknowledges it. The conversation continues.
- This IS the Delia goodbye. Not words. Not a scene. An exit no one saw.
- The reader catches it. Amber doesn't. (Or catches it only later, looking back.)

**What Amber feels:** Warmth from the table. Connection with the kids and Oak. She doesn't register Delia's absence because she's used to it — Delia has been leaving rooms for years.

**Craft notes:**
- The Delia exit should be ONE line buried in the flow of conversation. Not dramatized. Not a paragraph break. Just... she's gone, and the next line of dialogue is Ash arguing with Gary about something. The reader has to catch it themselves.
- Economy: the dinner chatter is fun AND it shows who Amber's real connections are (Oak, Ash, Gary — not Delia).
- Don't moralize. Don't have Amber reflect on Delia's absence. Just let the empty chair exist.

## Scene 6: Packing / Quiet Close

**Setting:** Amber's room at Delia's house. Night. Everyone's gone home or gone to bed.

**The room:** Hers but not hers. She's slept here for years but probably hasn't personalized it much. A bed, functional furniture, whatever Delia provided. Clean. Impersonal. A guest room occupied long-term.

**What happens:**
- Amber packs. Gear from Oak's lab already organized — this is final assembly. Practical, competent.
- The notebook goes in the bag. Her most important possession. Close to her body.
- Ditto is with her (came home from the ranch). The one comfortable presence.
- A moment of stillness: the house is quiet around her. Delia is somewhere behind a closed door. Ash is asleep. Tomorrow this room is empty.

**What she feels:** Not nostalgia. Not anxiety. Anticipation. The calm of someone who's been ready for months and the bureaucracy finally caught up. Tomorrow she's herself.

**Length:** Short. Half a page to a page. The quietness IS the content. Let it breathe.

**Craft notes:**
- The notebook going into the bag is a callback to Scene 1 (snapping it shut at school). Her private world, portable now.
- Don't narrate what she's leaving behind. The sparse room says it.
- Ditto's presence = she's not lonely. She has what she needs.

## Scene 7: Departure

**Setting:** Morning. Delia's front door → Route 1.

**Entry:** Simple. Morning light. Pack on her back. Ditto on her shoulder (or in blob form, or as scarf). She walks out the front door.

**What matters:** It's legitimate. Licensed. Nobody is chasing her. Nobody is stopping her. The door closes and she walks toward Route 1.

**Possible small beats:**
- The house behind her, quiet. No one at the window (or: Delia at the window, but Amber doesn't look back to see).
- The physical sensation of the pack, the morning air, the road opening up.
- Ditto doing something small — shifting, settling, a familiar weight.
- The transition from Pallet's residential quiet to Route 1's edge.

**What she feels:** Forward. Not running. Not looking back — not because it hurts, but because there's nothing back there pulling her. The road is the first thing in years that's fully, uncomplicated hers.

**End of chapter:** Something small that signals momentum. Not grand. Not a declaration. A step, a breath, a shift in the quality of light. The beginning of something real.

**Craft notes:**
- Resist the urge to make this a Big Moment. The whole chapter has been building to this — the departure doesn't need to announce itself. The quieter it is, the more it lands.
- No reflective summary ("And so I left Pallet Town, the place that had been..."). She just walks.
- The kaomoji section break could return here — signaling to the reader that we're in lighter territory now. Arc 1 begins.

## What This Chapter Is NOT

- Not a trauma chapter. She's past it.
- Not a reconciliation chapter. Delia and Amber don't resolve anything here.
- Not an exposition chapter. No long explanations of the licensing system, the exam, the world.
- Not dramatic. The whole point is that leaving is EASY — and that ease tells us everything about what her life has been.
