# Chapter 27 Plan: The Grass Dungeon Proposition (Working Title)

**POV:** Amber (1st person)
**Timeline:** After Chapter 26, on Route 2 / at a camp
**Location:** Route 2, then the approach to an off-route known Mystery Dungeon

## Chapter Purpose and Reader Effect

Turn Amber and Alonso's next shared movement into an elective choice rather
than a contract. A prepared Mystery Dungeon expedition contrasts with the
forced collapse-survival experience of Chapters 23--24, and makes its safety
cost feel routine but burdensome. The reader should feel the practical pain of
roster limits when Amber releases the newly caught Pidgey to open the slot she
expects to use for a Grass-type answer to Pewter. The capture payoff is
Heracross, not a separate Grass Pokemon; the Dungeon's primary emotional job
remains Amber and Alonso choosing the expedition and each other.

## Beat-by-Beat Intention

1. **Reunite at Route 2 or a camp.** Amber and Alonso meet again after their
   Chapter 26 separation. Their connection resumes through practical shared
   direction, not a formal request to become permanent companions.
2. **Offer the Grass Dungeon opportunity.** Alonso knows of a more
   level-appropriate, off-route Mystery Dungeon with a Grass-oriented
   ecosystem. Amber initially sees it as a chance to find a Grass-type answer
   to the coming Pewter Rock Gym; Ditto, Caterpie, Teddiursa, and Pidgey are a
   poor fit for that matchup.
3. **Frame this entry as voluntary preparation.** Unlike the collapse in
   Chapter 22 and survival Dungeon of Chapters 23--24, this is a known,
   regulated site they choose to approach. Escape Orbs are ordinary required
   safety equipment for that kind of deliberate entry, not a new magic trick.
4. **Make the recurring cost visible.** A working Escape Orb costs ₽300;
   a standard Pokeball costs ₽200. The Orb should feel like expensive
   expedition overhead, especially because Dungeon trips may repeat. Do not
   settle its exact operation in scene exposition.
5. **Confront the full roster before entering.** Amber has four active
   Pokemon, the limit allowed by her first badge. The prospective Rock-counter
   capture requires an open active slot; no instantaneous universal PC transfer
   resolves this decision.
6. **Release Pidgey.** Amber releases the Ch26 Pidgey deliberately. The bond
   is new and shallower than Caterpie's or Teddiursa's, but the release still
   hurts. Her rationale is that Pidgey are common and she expects she could
   catch another; preserve the thought as intent, never as approved wording.
7. **Set the next capture payoff.** The Grass Dungeon after this setup is the
   settled site of Amber's conventional conscious-battle capture of Heracross.
   It charges the group with the confidence its species reputation warrants, is
   easily overpowered, gets up, and charges again. This is not a Caterpie- or
   Teddiursa-style choice capture; do not pull the battle forward or settle its
   exact choreography here.
8. **Set the chosen-companion pattern.** The elective expedition is the next
   choice in a progression: forced cooperation (Ch21--24), shared Chapter 25,
   temporary parting (Ch26), chosen expedition (Ch27), then same-direction
   travel becoming normal. Do not force a definitive label or formal pact.

## Iceberg Facts

- Deliberate entry into regulated/known Mystery Dungeons generally requires an
  Escape Orb as a safety precaution. Entering without one is a civil
  fine/safety violation comparable to a seat-belt fine, not automatically a
  serious crime.
- Rangers patrol known Dungeon outskirts and entrances, may inspect Trainer
  IDs, custody compliance, and required safety gear. Accidental or forced
  entry such as the Chapter 22 collapse is not a violation.
- The author-set working price is ₽300 per Escape Orb and ₽200 per standard
  Pokeball. The economic relationship, not a currency conversion lesson, is
  what matters in this chapter.
- Escape Orb mechanics remain unchosen: single-use status, party radius,
  failure modes, backup requirements, and behavior in unstable/sealed sites
  are not current canon.
- Amber's first badge permits four active Pokemon. Her roster at this point is
  Ditto, Caterpie, Teddiursa, and the Chapter 26 Pidgey. The full-six threshold
  is four badges; active custody never exceeds six.
- The Pidgey loss is the fourth distinct beat in the running thread, not proof
  that Pidgey is Amber's favorite: Ch19's target fainted before capture; Ch20's
  different Pidgey was caught and Ch21's recovery broke its thin new bond;
  Ch26's Pidgey is caught; Ch27's Pidgey is released for roster space.
- A release is a real custody change, not a transfer into ubiquitous digital
  storage. Existing custody/fainting rules remain in force.
- The settled capture outcome is Heracross, a Bug/Fighting Pokemon. Bug's
  weakness to Rock and Fighting's resistance to Rock leave Rock damage neutral;
  Fighting offense is super-effective against Rock. A physical Grass attack
  such as *Bullet Seed* would be 4x effective against likely Rock/Ground
  opponents, but no move, source, or timing is settled.

## Constraints

- Do not decide the Dungeon's exact contents, level/rating, loot, duration,
  encounter sequence, or the battle/capture choreography. Heracross's capture
  there is settled; do not call Heracross a Grass Pokemon.
- Do not prescribe Escape Orb mechanics beyond its settled safety role and
  ₽300 price, or imply a separate explorer license exists.
- Do not make Pidgey's release callous, a favorite-Pokemon declaration, or an
  exact interior line. It can expose pragmatic game-mindedness without
  settling Amber as cruel.
- Do not convert the reunion into an immediate permanent-companion contract.
- Do not contradict the active-ball/fainting rule or solve the decision with
  general instantaneous PC transfer.

## Unresolved Decisions

- The Dungeon's rating, contents, legal access procedure, loot, length, and
  actual expedition outcome.
- Escape Orb mechanics: single-use, scope/radius, failure cases, and required
  redundancy.
- Whether a separate explorer qualification, permit, or hazard rating exists
  beyond ordinary trainer licensing.
- Whether the Pidgey release occurs at the entry, at camp, or another moment
  that gives the decision its intended weight.
- The exact chapter placement of the Dungeon entry and Heracross capture after
  this setup.
- Teddiursa is settled as Amber's active third Pokemon after Ch. 25, but the
  exact conventional capture/registration timing must be reconciled with the
  Chapter 21--25 continuity before drafting.

## Provenance

### Author-set / settled

- Route 2/camp reunion; Alonso offers a Grass-Dungeon opportunity before Pewter.
- Known-Dungeon entry is voluntary and prepared, contrasting the Ch23 survival
  Dungeon.
- Escape Orbs are generally required for deliberate regulated/known Dungeon
  entry and cost ₽300; a Pokeball costs ₽200.
- Amber's full four-Pokemon roster forces a Pidgey release to open a prospective
  Grass-type Rock-counter slot; she catches Heracross there instead. The release
  hurts despite the shallow new bond.
- Alonso and Amber become proper companions through repeated chosen
  cooperation, not an immediate declaration.
