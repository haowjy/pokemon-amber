# \[Chapter 12\] Sweet Home

As the sound of tires faded, Ash's father gently rested a hand on my shoulder and guided me into the house. A worn lab coat hung by the door. The house smelled of curry and clean laundry.

A display case mounted above the fireplace caught my eye: eight badges gleaming under glass, arranged with care. Below them, Ash had spread cards---Pokemon cards?---across the coffee table in a matching pattern, Poliwag waddling between the neat rows with surprising care.

"Sit," Ash's father said from the kitchen doorway. "I'm Stephen, by the way. Are you hungry? We have leftover curry."

I took a seat at the kitchen table.

My first instinct was to refuse politely. My stomach growled before I could. The last thing I'd eaten was a bowl of soggy cereal that morning.

Stephen chuckled as he heard my stomach growl.

Without waiting for a proper response, he pulled two containers from the fridge---curry in one, neatly packed rice in the other. As he leaned in, a Pokeball swung free from his shirt collar, hanging from a worn chain around his neck.

As he assembled the plate, the smell of rich spices filled the kitchen.

"It might need reheating. It's been in the fridge for a bit," Stephen said. "Delia says the curry needs to be properly hot to bring out the flavors. Unless you prefer it this way?"

I shrugged, watching as he placed the plate in the microwave.

As the plate rotated slowly behind the glass, my eyes drifted to those badges gleaming above the fireplace. The question slipped out before I could stop it: "Were you a trainer?"

Stephen touched something beneath his shirt collar. "A lifetime ago."

"What happened?"

He looked at the badge display reflected in the kitchen window. "I wasn't a good enough trainer back then. But I think now we..."

His voice trailed off. In the quiet that followed, I could hear the hum of the microwave and, through the window, the snip of Delia's shears.

The microwave dinged. Stephen set the steaming plate in front of me, then rolled his sleeves back up, returning to the sink where soap suds climbed toward his elbows as he tackled what looked like a particularly stubborn pot.

The curry was surprisingly good. The onions had been cooked until they almost melted, and the warmth of the spices lingered after each bite. Did Delia cook like this in the anime? I vaguely remembered something about her running a restaurant, but couldn't place it. I took another spoonful.

When I finished, Stephen dried his hands and reached into a high cabinet. "Here," he said, offering me the cookie with a gentle smile. "You look like you could use something sweet."

"Hey!" A young voice made me jump. Ash stood in the kitchen doorway, Poliwag peering around his legs. "That's not fair! Mom said I couldn't have any cookies tonight! Why does she get them?"

"Ash," Stephen started, but Ash was already moving closer. "That's because you and Poliwag tracked mud into... let her---"

"Who are you anyway?" Ash demanded, turning to me. "Why're you eating our cookies?"

"Poli pol!" Poliwag added, waddling over to investigate the cookie crumbs.

I looked down at the cookie, then at Ash's frown, then back at the cookie. I broke it in half, keeping the slightly smaller piece for myself.

"Here." I held out half, though I wanted to pull it back and eat the whole thing. "Want... to share?"

Ash's eyes widened. He glanced at Stephen, who checked where Delia was working in the garden before giving a small nod.

"Thanks!" Ash dropped into the chair next to me, cookie already halfway to his mouth. "I'm Ash, and that's Poliwag. He's not officially mine yet 'cause I'm not old enough, but he will be. What's your name?"

The cookie went dry in my mouth. That simple question again. But my name... was it the one from college? From my previous life? The identity Dr. Fuji had tried to force on me? Or something new?

I looked away from him, out through the window. Delia was still at work, her shadow stretching across the flowerbeds.

"She's had a long day, Ash," Stephen said. "Why don't you tell her about that new move you've been teaching Poliwag instead?"

"Oh man, you should see what Poliwag can do! We've been practicing Water Gun for weeks! Well, mostly inside 'cause of the rain, which is why we're in trouble, but---" Ash bounced up from his chair. "Can I show her, Dad? Please? Just a tiny demonstration?"

"Outside only," Stephen said firmly, though I caught the hint of a smile. "And aim away from your mother's flowers."

"Yes! Come on!" Ash was already heading for the door, Poliwag bouncing at his heels. He turned back. "You coming?"

"Sure." I followed them out, cookie crumbs still stuck to my fingers.

Delia straightened from her flower beds as we passed. The shears stilled in her hands, and her eyes followed us toward the yard.

Ash charged ahead to a worn patch of grass beyond the garden, Poliwag bouncing at his heels. I followed them past small pools and patches of mud left by earlier Water Gun practice.

Behind us, Stephen paused beside Delia. Their voices dropped too low to hear, and I turned back to Ash as he got into position.

"Okay, Poliwag!" Ash threw his arms wide with childish drama.

Poliwag was already bouncing forward, too excited to wait for actual commands. The Water Gun burst out in an enthusiastic but uncontrolled spray, sending both of us scrambling backward, laughing.

"See? I told you he was strong!" Ash bounced in place, soaked but grinning. "Dad's been helping us practice aim, but Poliwag gets too excited sometimes. Right, buddy?"

"Poli!" The Pokemon nodded enthusiastically, sending water droplets flying.

I wiped water off my face, still laughing. "How long have you been training together?"

"Three whole months!" He dropped beside Poliwag and scratched under its chin. "Since the river incident. Poliwag tried to save me, and Dad caught him after." He grinned through the entire account of his own near-disaster. "Now we train here where it's safe. Plus, Dad says it's good practice---Poliwag's gonna be my official starter once I'm old enough!"

"I'm gonna be the greatest Pokemon Master ever!" Ash declared between Water Gun attempts, fist pumped toward the orange sky. "Once I'm old enough, me and Poliwag will travel everywhere and I'll catch 'em all!"

"Poli pol!" Poliwag bounced proudly, agreeing with Ash.

Behind us, Stephen and Delia spoke too quietly for me to hear.

I focused instead on Ash's increasingly chaotic "special moves" demonstration, each attempt ending in more splashing and laughter.

The sunset turned the flying water droplets orange and gold. Looking at this soaking wet kid with impossible dreams, I couldn't help but smile.

In the story I knew, he would chase that dream across region after region, face down legendary Pokemon, and inspire millions of fans along the way. But this world had already given him a father and a Poliwag. Maybe it would give him a different journey too.

"Time to come in!" Stephen's voice cut through the twilight. "Before you both catch colds."

Ash made a token protest, but Poliwag was already waddling toward the house, leaving tiny wet footprints on the stepping stones. I followed, suddenly aware of how my dress clung damply to my skin.

Inside, the kitchen lights cast warm pools across water-spotted floors. Stephen appeared with an armful of towels, tossing one to Ash before holding another out to me. "Did you have fun?"

I nodded and patted my face dry. The towel smelled of sunshine and fresh air.

"Ash, shower time," Stephen said, collecting the wet towels. "And don't forget to dry Poliwag properly this time."

"But Dad, he's a water-type!"

"No buts. You know the rules about wet Pokemon in bed."

Ash thundered up the stairs, Poliwag bouncing after him with surprising grace for something shaped like a beach ball with legs. Their footsteps crossed the ceiling, followed by the shower starting.

I sank into a chair at the kitchen table while Stephen rinsed my curry plate. Now that Ash was upstairs, I wasn't sure what to say. I studied the calendar by the drying rack instead. Ash had covered it with notes on Poliwag's training.

Stephen finished with the plate and dried his hands on a dishtowel. He turned toward me, adjusting the chain around his neck, but waited until Ash's singing started overhead before he spoke.

"So," he said. "Do you... remember anything? About before?"

'What a loaded question.'

Above us, Ash belted out what sounded like the Pokemon theme song, completely off-key and completely happy. Through the window, Delia's shadow moved across the garden one last time.

"Delia told me some things," Stephen continued when I didn't answer. "About how Amber---her previous daughter---died. About how Dr. Fuji became... obsessed." He leaned against the counter and studied the worn linoleum. "I never thought he'd actually come back. Not like this... not with a... you." He looked at me, then away.

I traced a scratch through the table's surface. How could I explain that bringing back Amber hadn't been enough? Every detail of my existence still had to match the memory he carried.

"Is there anything you need?" Stephen asked quietly. "To feel more comfortable here?"

I stared at the scratch on the table, my finger stopped at a corner in its path.

A couple weeks ago, in my dorm room, I would've just said time---more time to play Pokemon games.

"I don't know," I muttered.

Stephen nodded. Above us, Ash's muffled voice switched from singing to a passionate discussion with Poliwag about proper towel usage.

He pushed away from the counter. "You'll stay in the guest room tonight. It's small, but..." He opened a cabinet. "And since Ash isn't here to claim his share---want another cookie?"

I nodded. I really wanted another cookie.

He handed me the cookie, then glanced at my mud-spattered dress. "You should probably wash up once Ash is done. Don't want to go to bed like a Mudkip, right? Just... let us know if you need anything, okay?"

I nodded, slowly nibbling on the satisfyingly sweet cookie.
