# Chapter 26 Plan: Leaving Viridian (Working Title)

**POV:** Amber (1st person)
**Timeline:** Immediately after Chapter 25
**Location:** Viridian's departure corridor and the Route 1 edge

## Chapter Purpose and Reader Effect

Move Amber out of Giovanni's physical and institutional radius without making
her feel free of its consequences. The reader should see ordinary regulation
as proportionate and fallible: a Ranger checks a visible custody loophole,
scolds the children who exploit it, clears Amber's valid paperwork, and lets
her continue. The final Pidgey catch restores a modest, deliberate success
while setting up a real roster cost rather than a permanent reward.

## Beat-by-Beat Intention

1. **Finish only the necessary Viridian aftermath.** Amber leaves promptly
   after Chapter 25; any remaining administration is brief and does not turn
   the chapter into a registration procedure. Her urgency is to get beyond
   Giovanni/Viridian's radius, not to narrate a new long-term plan.
2. **Temporarily part from Alonso.** They do not sign up as permanent travel
   companions or make a formal partnership declaration. Their forced
   cooperation in Chapters 21--24 and shared Chapter 25 have made future
   cooperation plausible; this chapter gives each room to choose it later.
3. **Meet the rich Youngster group's pooled-team battle.** A wealthy Youngster
   leader battles Amber while several other wealthy children each carry one
   legal Pokemon. The group effectively pools those Pokemon under the
   leader's direction: a spirit-of-law / gray-area workaround, not the old
   single child openly carrying an illegal second Pokemon.
4. **Make the Ranger intervention a pressure turn, not an action climax.** A
   Ranger has arrived or been watching during the battle. When the leader is
   about to reveal a second pooled Pokemon, he ends the match and presents the
   loss as his single-Pokemon loss. Keep the scene function clear without
   prescribing dialogue.
5. **Inspect and proportionately resolve.** The Ranger checks every child's
   Trainer ID and the group’s custody situation, scolds the pooling behavior,
   and does not bring a serious charge. The precise statutory basis is open.
   The Ranger checks Amber's valid license and four-Pokemon custody allowance,
   then lets her go. Amber leaves quickly rather than lingering for a victory
   lap.
6. **Catch a Pidgey by an ordinary conscious battle capture.** Away from the
   inspection, Amber deliberately reproduces a kind of capture that earlier
   circumstances denied her. The capture fills her fourth active slot:
   Ditto, Caterpie, Teddiursa, and Pidgey. Its practical mechanics are a
   drafting decision; it must not contradict the conscious-target capture and
   fainting rules.
7. **Leave the Pidgey thread unresolved.** The species carries generic
   early-Pokemon nostalgia for Amber, not declared favorite status. The catch
   is intentionally shallow compared with Caterpie and Teddiursa, so the next
   chapter can make roster space painful without pretending the bond is equal.

## Iceberg Facts

- A trainer license permits Amber to carry three active Pokemon; Giovanni's
  first Gym Badge raises her active custody limit to four. Four badges, not
  six, is the full-six threshold; active custody never exceeds six.
- An unlicensed person may carry one personal Pokemon. More than one carried
  or actively controlled Pokemon needs a trainer license and is subject to
  badge custody limits.
- Rangers patrol maintained routes and access points, may inspect Trainer IDs,
  carried Pokemon/custody compliance, and safety gear, and generally handle
  minor violations proportionately. They should recur occasionally, not every
  chapter.
- A fainted Pokemon cannot be newly captured or recalled: its active ball
  connection is broken until recovery. Established partners normally relink
  after recovery; a thin new bond can fail to do so, as the Chapter 21 Pidgey
  did.
- Amber's Pidgey pattern is intentional: the Chapter 19 target fainted before
  capture; the different Chapter 20 Pidgey was caught, then left and flew away
  after recovery in Chapter 21; this Chapter 26 Pidgey is the next attempt.
- There is no ubiquitous instantaneous PC transfer that can dissolve this
  choice. Limited transfer service exists only through select, approved
  facilities and has not solved the immediate road roster problem.

## Constraints

- Do not use the superseded scene in which one child with two Pokemon runs and
  Tangela physically captures him.
- Do not write approved-sounding dialogue. The leader's "one Pokemon" claim
  is scene function only, not settled wording.
- Do not make the children dangerous criminals or make the Ranger brutal;
  they are affluent children exploiting a gray area and receive a scolding.
- Do not make the Pidgey capture a Caterpie- or Teddiursa-style relationship
  choice, or prescribe a ball tactic beyond a conscious conventional capture.
- Do not make Alonso's regional circuit enrollment a completed fact. If it is
  mentioned, present it as administration he might pursue, not a settled
  process.

## Unresolved Decisions

- What, if anything, remains to be handled administratively after Chapter 25?
- Does Alonso formally enroll in the Indigo Gym circuit after receiving
  Giovanni's badge, and what recognition process applies to his foreign
  identity? This is plausible but unchosen.
- Does the Ranger have enough statutory authority to fine pooled-team conduct,
  or is the scolding purely a response to gray evidence? The settled scene
  result is no serious charge.
- What exact battle/capture mechanics make the Pidgey conscious and catchable?
- Teddiursa is settled as Amber's active third Pokemon after Ch. 25, but the
  exact conventional capture/registration moment must be reconciled with the
  Chapter 21--25 material before prose is drafted.

## Provenance

### Author-set / settled

- Immediate departure from Viridian; temporary separation from Alonso.
- Rich Youngster leader plus peers pooling individually legal Pokemon; Ranger
  sees the issue, checks documents, scolds, clears Amber, and Amber leaves.
- First badge raises Amber from three to four active Pokemon; she then catches
  Pidgey by a conventional conscious battle capture.
- Pidgey's early-franchise nostalgia is generic rather than favoritism, and
  the repeated inability to keep one is an intentional running pattern.
